### `public/images/research/sciml-hybrid-flash-point.png`

Maurício Prado de Omena Souza, Diego Tavares Volpatto, Antonio Marinho Barbosa Neto and Mariana Conceição da Costa, “Modeling flash points of biofuels using thermodynamically consistent neural networks,” *Fluid Phase Equilibria* 605, 114673 (2026). [DOI: 10.1016/j.fluid.2026.114673](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fluid.2026.114673).

**Copyright © The Authors 2026. Published by Elsevier B.V. under [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)**, as stated on the first page of the supplied article. Figure 3 on PDF page 5 was extracted in full from its embedded image at the original 1505 × 1001 pixel resolution. No changes were made to the figure, its equations, arrows or labels. The figure depicts a neural flash-point model with the Liaw-equation residual and external vapor-pressure/activity-coefficient models entering the training loss. Attribution belongs to all four article authors, not solely to the website owner.

The SciML description also draws on the ODML study by Kyas et al. (2022), the [GibbsFlashNN supporting material](https://github.com/ThermoPhase-FCSRG/gibbsflashnn-supporting-material) and the [torch-flash documentation](https://thermophase-fcsrg.github.io/torch-flash/). It distinguishes learning on demand from neural thermodynamic models and does not equate a reduced physical residual with improved predictive accuracy or a global-equilibrium guarantee. The 2026 flash-point article reports that adding physical constraints did not systematically improve predictive RMSE; no universal accuracy or speed-up claim is made on the website.
